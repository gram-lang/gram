Three different things are easy to mix up when you read a recipe: **what you need**, **the work of getting it ready**, and **when you do that work**. Gram keeps them apart on purpose, and only the last one is a choice.

If you cook a simple recipe, you can stop here: nothing to configure, the defaults do what you expect. The rest of this page is for those who want to plan a complex recipe, with several components or several days.

## Three ideas, one choice

| | What it is | Does the planning choice change it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Ingredients** | What the recipe needs: the ingredient list of each section, the shopping list | No |
| **Mise en place** | The work of getting it ready: fetching, weighing, peeling, chopping. Each section reports its own cost (`miseEnPlace`) | No |
| **Planning** | When that work happens: the timeline, the total time, the idle time and the Gantt chart | **Yes**, this is the only thing it changes |

The recipe never declares how it should be planned. It states facts (what you need, what preparing it costs), and **you** choose how to lay them out in time, depending on how you work.

This gives one rule that is easy to remember: choosing a planning never changes the ingredient lists, the shopping list, the preparation time or the active time. It only moves the work along the timeline.

## The three plannings

Gram plans the mise en place in three ways. Take this recipe:

```gram
## Dough ->&dough

Mix @flour{500g} and @water{300ml} in a #bowl.

[Rest] Let it rest ~_{2h}.

## Filling

Slice @onions{200g}(finely sliced).

## Assembly

Combine &dough and the filling, then bake for ~{25min}.
```

Its preparation costs 7 minutes in all (3 for the dough, 3 for the filling, 1 for the assembly) and its active time is 29 minutes, whichever way it is planned.

**By section** (the default) puts each section's mise en place right before that section, and lets it overlap the resting time of an earlier step:

- The dough is prepared at minute 0, then rests until minute 125.
- The filling's mise en place happens at the end of the rest, at minute 119, so the onions don't wait on the counter.
- The total time is 2 h 30 (150 min).

**All at the start** does all the mise en place first, then starts cooking:

- The dough's and the filling's mise en place both happen from minute 0, before the first step.
- The total time is 2 h 33 (153 min): the filling's preparation is no longer absorbed by the dough's rest.

**By working day** groups the mise en place by day of work. The recipe above has no section anchored a day or more back (`~{-1d}`, `~{-2d}`...), so it is a single day, and this planning is the same as "all at the start". It only differs for a recipe over several days, see below.

None is "right". The first reads like cooking one component after the other; the second reads like a working session where everything is laid out before you start; the third, like one production sheet per day.

### An intermediate is never prepared before it exists

An intermediate (`&dough`) is made during the recipe, so it can't be fetched or weighed before the section that makes it has run. This holds in all three plannings:

- It is counted in the mise en place of the section that **uses** it, not the one that makes it.
- Even when everything else is prepared at the start, it is planned right before the section that uses it. In the example, `&dough` is gathered at minute 127, just after it has rested, in the "all at the start" planning.

So a recipe planned "all at the start" is really a mix: the raw ingredients and the cookware first, and the intermediates as they become available. That is also how a kitchen works.

:::note
That one minute to gather an intermediate is an approximation: it can fall while the intermediate is still resting. The total time stays right to within a minute.
:::

## Where to choose

The choice is made by the person reading the recipe, in the tool they use:

| Tool | How |
|---|---|
| CLI (`gram view`, `gram export`, `gram print`) | `--mise-en-place <per-section\|upfront\|per-session>` |
| VS Code | the `gram.miseEnPlace` setting |
| Playground | the selector in the playground |
| Renderer (`@gram-lang/renderer`) | the `schedule` option (`"perSection"`, `"upfront"` or `"perSession"`) |

If you use the compiler directly, `miseEnPlace` holds the facts and `schedules` holds the three plannings (`perSection`, `upfront` and `perSession`). Read the one you want, and never rebuild the timeline from `miseEnPlace` yourself. See the [kitchen API reference](/docs/reference/api/kitchen/) for the exact shape.

## Recipes over several days

A recipe over several days is worked in sessions: on each day you prepare what that day needs. The **by working day** planning gives one production sheet per day, with no new syntax and nothing to configure beyond the retro-planning anchors you already write.

```gram
## Cream ~{-3d} ->&cream

Whisk @milk{500ml} and @eggs{4}.

[Chill] Let it set ~_{24h}.

## Crust ~{-26h} ->&crust

Work &cream{150g} with @flour{300g} and @butter{150g}.

## Pie ~{-24h}

Fill with &crust{400g} and bake ~{30min}.

## Serve

Dust with @sugar{10g}.
```

### Where the days come from

The days come from the section anchors, nothing else:

- A section anchored `~{-Nd}` or more (in hours too: `~{-36h}` is one day back, `~{-12h}` is the day itself) sits on that day. The count rounds down.
- A section without an anchor sits on the day of the **next anchor** in the recipe, like in [ALAP scheduling](/docs/explanation/alap-scheduling/), where each anchor is a deadline.
- Sections after the last anchor sit on the day itself.

Two things don't open a day, and trip people up:

- **A long passive timer.** A `~_{48h}` rest in a step makes [ALAP scheduling](/docs/explanation/alap-scheduling/) start the step two days earlier on the Gantt, but the days only read section anchors: without a `~{-2d}` on the section's title, the section is on the day itself. Anchor the section (`## Cream ~{-2d}`).
- **An anchor under 24 hours.** `~{-18h}` rounds down to 0 days, so a poolish kneaded the evening before is on the day itself, not on D-1. To open the day before, use `~{-1d}` or more than 24 hours (`~{-26h}`).

In the example: the cream is on D-3, the crust and the pie on D-1, and the last section on the day itself. Without any anchor of a day or more, there is a single session, like "all at the start".

### What each day gathers

At the start of each day, the mise en place of that day's sections is gathered: ingredients, cookware and preparations. For an intermediate:

- made on an **earlier day**, it is gathered at the start of the day that uses it (the cream, on D-1);
- made **the same day**, it waits until right before the section that uses it (the crust, for the pie), because it doesn't exist yet when the day starts.

The preparation time and the active time stay the same as in the other plannings.

:::tip
When several sections of the same day depend on each other, space their anchors a little (`~{-26h}`, `~{-25h}`, `~{-24h}`) rather than giving them all `~{-1d}`. Each anchor is a deadline, so two sections with the same deadline where one needs the other produce a time paradox warning.
:::
