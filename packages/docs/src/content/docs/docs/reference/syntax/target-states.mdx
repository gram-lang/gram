A time says how long a step should take; it is rarely what tells the cook it is done. Custard is ready when it coats the spoon, egg yolks and sugar when they fall back in a ribbon, butter when it smells of hazelnut. A <Term id="targetState" link={false} /> marks that cue with `={...}`: the state to reach, the one the cook has to recognize.

```gram
Whisk @egg yolks{4} with @sugar{80 g} until ={ribbon stage}.

As soon as the butter turns ={nutty brown}, take the #pan off the heat.

The egg whites should be ={stiff} before you add the @sugar{50 g}.
```

A target state goes wherever the sentence needs it. It is most often at the end of the step, since it usually marks what ends it, but it can also open the step or sit in the middle, and no phrase is required: "until", "as soon as", "when", or nothing at all. Write only the state between the braces and leave the other words in the sentence: the step still reads naturally, and the state stands out on its own in every view.

## How to check it

A note in parentheses, right after the closing brace, says how to tell that the state is reached: the test, the cue a beginner can trust.

```gram
Cook on ^{low heat}, stirring, until ={it coats the spoon}(a finger drawn across the back of the spoon leaves a clean line) ->&custard{}
```

The state and its note are always shown, on screen and on paper, and `gram cook` repeats them under the step. They are words for the cook, in the recipe's language: Gram keeps them as written and never reads them.

As with ingredients, cookware, and temperatures, keep the opening `(` glued to the `}` on the same line: with a space (`={stiff peaks} (about 5 min)`), the parenthesis stays ordinary text in the sentence.

## With a time and a temperature

A step can carry a target state next to a timer and a temperature. The timer is the estimate, for the schedule; the state is what ends the step at the stove.

```gram
Cook at ^{82°C}(internal) for ~{8 min}, until ={it coats the spoon}.
```

:::tip[What does not go inside]
A target state is a state, never a measure:
- a temperature to reach is a [temperature](/docs/reference/syntax/temperatures/) with its note: `^{63°C}(internal)`;
- a time is a [timer](/docs/reference/syntax/times/): `~{5 min}`;
- a target state is never scaled: `={doubled in volume}` stays as written, whatever the number of servings.
:::

## Several in one step

A step can hold more than one. Each shows where it was written, `gram cook` lists them one under the other and, when a timer of the step rings, asks to check them all.

```gram
Knead until the dough is ={smooth} and ={elastic}(it springs back slowly when stretched).
```

As with timers and temperatures, a short step is easier to follow. When each state ends a different action, make two steps, each with its own state. Rather than:

```gram
Melt the @butter{50 g} until ={nutty brown}, then let it cool until ={lukewarm} and stir in the @eggs{2}.
```

write:

```gram
Melt the @butter{50 g} until ={nutty brown}.

Let it cool until ={lukewarm}, then stir in the @eggs{2}.
```

## Errors

A target state needs words between its braces, closed on the same line. `={}` and `={ribbon stage` (with no `}`) are syntax errors, shown in the editor where they start.

An `=` that does not touch a brace is ordinary text, and the fixed-quantity modifier `@=salt{1 tsp}` is unchanged.

## In the compiled recipe

Each target state is a `checkpoint` token in the step's `content`, where it was written: `{ "type": "checkpoint", "state": "ribbon stage", "note": "…" }`, `note` only when there is one. See [Data formats](/docs/reference/api/data-formats/).
