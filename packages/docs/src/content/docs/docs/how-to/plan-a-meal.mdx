You want to serve a pistachio and raspberry entremets on Saturday at 8 p.m. It needs three days of work, a sponge and an insert made two days before, a glaze you may already have in the freezer, and you can cook between 8 a.m. and 10 p.m. This guide goes from the recipe to a plan you can follow in the kitchen and put in your calendar.

The recipe says **how** to do things. The work plan says **when to come** and **what to take out**. Gram computes it from the recipe, so there is nothing to write twice.

The files of this guide are in [`public/examples/work-plan/`](https://git.gram-lang.org/gram-lang/gram/src/branch/main/packages/docs/public/examples/work-plan): `entremets.gram` imports three bases with `@use`.

<Steps>

1. ### Write the waits as ranges and anchor the days

   A wait written as a range (`~_{12-72h}`) can be longer or shorter, and Gram uses that margin to fit your availability. A day anchor (`@use "..." as &sponge ~{-2d}` or `## Mousse ~{-1d}`) says on which day a part of the recipe is done.

   ```gram
   ---
   title: Pistachio raspberry entremets
   portions: 6
   ---

   @use "./bases/pistachio-sponge.gram" as &sponge ~{-2d}
   @use "./bases/raspberry-insert.gram" as &insert ~{-2d}
   @use "./bases/white-chocolate-glaze.gram" as &glaze

   ## Mousse ->&mousse ~{-1d}

   [Whip] Whip @cream{250g} with @sugar{30g} and @milk{100g} using the #stand mixer{} ~{10min}.

   [Assemble] Layer &sponge, &insert and the mousse in the #20 cm ring{} ~{10min}, then freeze ~_{12-48h}.

   ## Finish

   [Unmould] Unmould the entremets and cover it with &glaze ~{5min}.

   [Thaw] Thaw in the fridge for ~_{6h}.
   ```

   See [Exact times and ranges](/docs/reference/syntax/times/#exact-times-and-ranges) and [Days and hours](/docs/reference/syntax/times/#days-and-hours).

2. ### Choose when to serve

   Not sure which evening works? Let Gram try each day at your meal times:

   ```bash
   gram plan entremets.gram --find 2026-10-15/2026-10-19 --meal-times 12:30,20:00 \
     --tz Europe/Paris --available 08:00-22:00
   ```

   ```text
   Thursday, October 15 20:00 — start Tue 13:45 · 3 visits to the kitchen · no wait adjusted
   Friday, October 16 20:00 — start Wed 13:45 · 3 visits to the kitchen · no wait adjusted
   Saturday, October 17 20:00 — start Thu 13:45 · 3 visits to the kitchen · no wait adjusted
   ...
   Thursday, October 15 12:30 — not possible. Mise en place — Sponge, around Tue 06:15, falls when you are not available, ... (+2)
   ```

   The times that work come first (fewer adjusted waits, then fewer visits, then the earliest). Each time that does not work says why. `--meal-times` replaces the default `12:30,20:00`; to set it once, add a `mealTimes` list to `.gram/config.yaml`.

   With no serving time at all, `gram plan entremets.gram` tells you the earliest it could be ready if you start now.

3. ### Get the work plan

   ```bash
   gram plan entremets.gram --serve "2026-10-17 20:00" --tz Europe/Paris --available 08:00-22:00 \
     --stock ./bases/white-chocolate-glaze.gram
   ```

   `--serve` is the local date and time, `--tz` the IANA time zone (set it once with `gram config set timezone Europe/Paris`), `--available` when you can cook (repeatable: `sat=09:00-23:00`, `2026-10-09=none`). `--stock` says that the glaze is already in your freezer: it is not planned, and shows up as "in stock".

   ```text
   Work plan — Pistachio raspberry entremets
   For Saturday, October 17 at 20:00 · 6 servings
   3 days · 3 visits to the kitchen · 45m of work in all

   THU 15   ▮ 13:45 – 14:00 Sponge, Insert
            ┆ Sponge : Bake 12m
            ┆ Insert : Freeze 12h to 72h
   FRI 16   ▮ 13:35 – 14:00 Mousse
            ┆ Mousse : Assemble 12h to 48h
   SAT 17   ▮ 13:55 – 14:00 Finish
            ┆ Finish : Thaw 6h
            ★ 20:00         Serve
   ```

4. ### Read it

   The top is the **planning of the days**: one line per visit to the kitchen (`▮`), with what goes on without you underneath (`┆`), and the service (`★`). Each day then has its own block:

   - **To take out**: the ingredients (summed when two sections need the same one: `sugar 90 g (60 + 30)`), then the preparations an earlier visit made (`Sponge, made Thursday`), a base in stock (`glaze, in stock`) and the cookware. Tick them as you go.
   - **To do**: one line per section, with the steps of the recipe it covers (`steps 1 to 2`, and the file for an import), then its actions, temperatures and timers as written. It does not repeat the recipe: it says where to read it.
   - **Before leaving**: what goes on after the visit, and when to come back.
   - **Possible from 08:00.** tells how early this visit could be done if everything else stays where it is. **Fixed times** names the wait that holds it in place.
   - A wait the plan made longer or shorter to fit your hours is reported at the top of its day.

   ```text
   FRIDAY, OCTOBER 16  13:35 → 14:00
     Possible from 08:00.
     To take out
       [ ] cream 250 g
       [ ] sugar 30 g
       [ ] milk 100 g
       [ ] Sponge, made Thursday
       [ ] Insert, made Thursday
       [ ] stand mixer
       [ ] 20 cm ring
     To do
       Mousse (steps 1 to 2) : Whip 10 min › Assemble 10 min · 12 to 48 h
     Before leaving
       Mousse : Assemble 12h to 48h, until 02:00
       Next visit: Saturday 13:55
   ```

   What the plan could not make work (a start already in the past, work outside your hours, a break longer than any of your availabilities) is listed under "To fix" at the top. See [Diagnostics and unresolved constraints](/docs/explanation/planning-in-real-time/#diagnostics-and-unresolved-constraints).

5. ### Print it with the recipe, or send it to your calendar

   ```bash
   gram print entremets.gram --serve "2026-10-17 20:00" --available 08:00-22:00 --with-sheet
   gram export entremets.gram --format md --serve "2026-10-17 20:00" --with-sheet
   gram plan entremets.gram --serve "2026-10-17 20:00" --available 08:00-22:00 --ics entremets.ics
   ```

   `--with-sheet` writes the work plan after the recipe, in one document (one day per page when printed, with check boxes). `--ics` writes one event per visit to the kitchen, with an alarm before it and the lists in its description. Events are in UTC: your calendar shows them in its own zone.

6. ### If you are late

   The plan is only true while you keep to it. If a visit slips past its time, run `gram plan` again with a later serving time, or with `--find` from now on: the plan is a function of what you give it, not a state.

7. ### Other formats

   `--format md` for Markdown, `--format html` for a fragment, `--format json` for the data (`plan` and `runSheet`, see [Data formats](/docs/reference/api/data-formats/#5-the-run-sheet)). The words follow the `language` of your project.

</Steps>

## In the playground

Open the studio ("Plan" button), enter a serving time, a time zone and your hours: the **Work plan** view shows the plan with dates. The "When to serve?" buttons set the time for you, and "Ready as early as" gives the earliest time from now.

## From code

```typescript
import { compile } from '@gram-lang/kitchen';
import { project, runSheet } from '@gram-lang/scheduler';
import { runSheetToText } from '@gram-lang/renderer';

const compiled = compile(ast);
const context = {
  serveAt: '2026-10-17T20:00',
  timeZone: 'Europe/Paris',
  availability: { daily: [{ start: '08:00', end: '22:00' }] },
};
const plan = project([{ graph: compiled.tasks, title: compiled.title ?? undefined }], context);

// With the graph and the context, each visit gets its margin.
const sheet = runSheet(plan, { graph: compiled.tasks, context });
console.log(runSheetToText(sheet, compiled));
```

The data is the point: the text above is one way to show it. See [Build a custom UI](/docs/how-to/build-custom-ui/) to draw your own.
