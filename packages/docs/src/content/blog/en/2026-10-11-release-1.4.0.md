Hi everyone! Gram `v1.4.0` is now available. This release is about **planning**: not how long a recipe takes, but when you should be in the kitchen, and what to put on the counter when you get there.

### The Gantt chart gives way to the work plan

The Gantt chart was one of the first views Gram had, and it shows. A 24-hour rest crushes a two-minute gesture, it scrolls sideways (hopeless on a tablet or a phone), and above all it answers a question nobody in a kitchen asks: *what does the whole timeline look like?*

**It is deprecated in 1.4.0 and removed in 2.0.0.** `toGanttHTML`, `attachGanttInteractivity`, `formatTime`, `formatAxisTime`, the `Gantt*` types, `@gram-lang/renderer/gantt.css`, the `gantt_*` strings, the `Gram: Gantt Chart` command and the `gram/ganttUpdated` notification all keep working until then, and `toGanttHTML` warns once on the console. No Gram surface draws it any more: use `runSheetToHTML()`, or build a view of your own from the data of `runSheet()`. The [Deprecated features](/docs/how-to/deprecations/) page has the before and after.

In its place, the **work plan** answers three questions, and nothing else:

1. **When do I need to be in the kitchen?** Which days, at what time, and what goes on without you between two visits.
2. **What do I take out?** A check list for each visit: the ingredients (following your mise en place mode: with each section, together for the working day, or all at the first visit), what an earlier visit made, a base you have in stock, the cookware.
3. **What do I do during this visit?** One line per section, with the steps of the recipe it covers.

```text
THU 15   ▮ 13:45 – 14:00 Sponge, Insert
         ┆ Insert : Freeze 12h to 72h
FRI 16   ▮ 13:35 – 14:00 Mousse
         ┆ Mousse : Assemble 12h to 48h
SAT 17   ▮ 13:55 – 14:00 Finish
         ★ 20:00         Serve
```

Nothing in it is invented: every name and quantity is the one of your `.gram` file, and every time is computed by the scheduler. The recipe says **how**; the plan says **when to come** and **what to take out**. It prints one day per page, with check boxes, and `gram export` and `gram print` can write it after the recipe (`--with-sheet`).

### Choosing when to serve

`gram plan recipe.gram --find 2026-10-12/2026-10-15` tries each day at your meal times and tells you which serving times work, best first, and why each of the others does not ("you would have to start Thursday"). Without a serving time at all, `gram plan` tells you the earliest it could be ready if you start now. The playground has the same: "When to serve?" and "Ready as early as".

Each visit also says how early it could be done ("Possible from 18:00"), or which exact wait holds it in place. The margin is safe by construction: everything is planned as late as possible, so a visit can only move earlier, and each wait around it is checked against the range you wrote.

### A scheduler of its own

The planning engine is now its own package, `@gram-lang/scheduler`, below the compiler: `layout()`, `project()`, `runSheet()`, `findServeTimes()` and `earliestServe()`. It knows nothing of the `.gram` syntax; it produces data, and the work plan, the playground and the editors are views of that data. A custom UI can draw its own: see [Build a custom UI](/docs/how-to/build-custom-ui/#build-your-own-planning-view).

### Other changes

- **Mise en place** can be planned right before each section, all at the start, or by working day (`--mise-en-place`, `gram.miseEnPlace`), and a wait written as a range follows `--rests`.
- **A new notice**, `PASSIVE_OVERLAP_SAME_COOKWARE`, flags two timers that overlap in the same oven without a named track in common.
- **A duration longer than 1000 years** no longer breaks the timeline: it is capped, with a warning.
- **Unused `--stock` entries** are reported on stderr by every command that takes it.

For the complete list of changes, check out the [Changelog](/docs/changelog/).
